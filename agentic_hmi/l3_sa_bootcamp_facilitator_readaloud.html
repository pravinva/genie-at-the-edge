How to run each round (repeat ×4)

  1. Unlock the round on ?gm=1 (or read aloud while they follow ?q=N).
  2. Read aloud the narrative below (1–2 min) — optional; they can also read the screen.
  3. Pair work (5–8 min): diagnosis on paper; agree on top 3 action letters.
  4. Share-outs (4–6 min): 2–3 pairs report letters + one sentence why.
  5. Reveal optimal set + talking points; debate 5–8 min using prompts below.
  6. Score in sidebar (+3 per correct letter, max 9/round) → next quarter.

Full session: ~55–80 min · Tight: ~45 min (shorter pair + debrief).

Opening (read once at start)

Read aloud You’re the Databricks SA on Meridian Health Systems, a fictional healthcare company — about twelve thousand employees, four billion dollars in revenue. They’re on AWS with Unity Catalog, Delta Lake, and Databricks SQL, roughly one point eight million in ARR against a two million dollar commitment, and they’ve been under-consuming for a while. Your champion is Dr. Priya Sharma, VP of Data. Your technical ally is Marcus Chen, platform lead. Aisha Johnson runs ML. Tom Kowalski is a skeptical data engineer who’s friendly to Snowflake. Rebecca Owens is the CFO who cares about spend. Over four quarters you’ll get new signals — champion risk, usage shifts, competitive noise, executive change. Each round: diagnose what’s really going on, then pick your top three actions from the menu. There’s no answer key on screen; we’ll debate ground truth after you commit. This is about judgment under partial information — when to discover versus pitch, when to bring SSA or Technical Services, and when not to escalate.

Account baseline (reference — don’t read verbatim unless helpful)

  • Flat consumption two quarters · 6 open support tickets · EBR ~5 months ago
  • Snowflake has met with analytics · Priya’s ROI story landed well with the board
  • ~40 all-purpose clusters, 15 job clusters, 3 SQL warehouses

Quarter 1 — The First Cracks

Read aloud after unlock It’s early in the year and three things hit at once. Marcus Slacks you: Priya just announced she’s leaving Meridian at the end of next month. She’s taking a role elsewhere. No replacement has been named. Your consumption dashboard shows a twelve percent drop in DBU usage this quarter. The drop is concentrated in the analytics organization — SQL warehouse queries are down significantly. And Marcus mentions in passing that Tom has been running benchmarks on a trial account with another vendor and has been pretty vocal about it in the engineering Slack channel. Three signals. One account. What’s connected? What’s noise? What do you do first?
Shape the discussion
  • Ask: “Are these three problems or one story?” — push pairs to link champion loss → political cover for analytics slowdown → Tom’s benchmarks.
  • Before reveal: “Who prioritized Priya’s exit window versus consumption versus Tom?”
  • Land the lesson: time-boxed champion risk + root-cause on SQL drop + hear Tom directly beat premature EBR, battle cards, or SSA deep-dives.
  • Anti-pattern callout: manager escalate, “do nothing,” or CI/UCO before you’ve learned anything.
Correct top 3: A · K · E (3 pts each · max 9)

A — 1:1 with Priya before she leaves (with AE): capture champions, succession, transition intel.
K — Discovery with analytics on the usage drop: SQL decline needs firsthand “why,” not guesses.
E — Intro to Tom via Marcus: engage competitive voice early through a trusted path.

Not this round: EBR (F), SSA deep-dive (D), TS health check (I), CI/UCO (G/H), manager panic (C), wait (J).

Quarter 2 — The Plot Thickens

Read aloud after unlock Priya is gone. Her replacement is David Park, a new VP of Data from an organization that ran heavily on Snowflake. He’s agreed to a thirty-minute intro call with you next week. Marcus warns you: David’s first question in his all-hands was why Meridian is spending two million dollars on Databricks when his last company did everything on Snowflake for less. Consumption ticked up five percent — but all of it is ML. Aisha’s team is running large-scale model training on Databricks. Meanwhile SQL warehouse usage dropped another eight percent. Marcus escalates: fourteen open support tickets, mostly Unity Catalog permission errors. His team spends twenty percent of their time on UC troubleshooting instead of building pipelines. New skeptical executive. ML bright spot. Analytics still sliding. Platform team drowning in permissions. What do you prioritize?
Shape the discussion
  • Optional 2-min brainstorm: “Top three discovery questions for David — no product names.”
  • Contrast discovery (B) vs Snowflake pitch deck (A/I) — David already suspects vendor spend.
  • Ask: “When is Technical Services warranted?” — 14 UC tickets + 20% time = pattern, not ticket-by-ticket Support.
  • ML SSA (L) before understanding Aisha’s growth (C) is premature.
Correct top 3: B · C · D

B — Discovery questions for David: listen before pitching.
C — Working session with Aisha / ML: nurture the growth engine and learn what’s next.
D — Technical Services UC health / architecture review: fix structural permission pain.

Not this round: Competitive deck (A/I), early EBR with CFO (H), SQL workshop before knowing why analytics left (E), Support ticket pile-on (K).

Quarter 3 — Turning Point

Read aloud after unlock Some good news: Technical Services found the UC problem — two hundred plus individual grants instead of group-based access. After two sessions, support tickets fell from fourteen to three. Marcus messages: “This is a game-changer. My team just got twenty percent of their time back.” David’s feedback after your intro call: he appreciated that you asked questions instead of pitching. He wants a model serving demo and a cost optimization review before he can justify platform spend to Rebecca. Consumption is flat overall. ML grew eighteen percent; SQL warehouse usage dropped another ten percent. Marcus tells you Tom has formally proposed migrating all analytics workloads to Snowflake — and the proposal is on David’s desk. Your session with Aisha’s team surfaced real expansion around feature engineering and model serving — work that needs ML depth beyond what you can carry solo. David gave you explicit asks. Tom went formal. ML is winning; analytics is still at risk. What’s your move?
Shape the discussion
  • Ask: “What does David optimize for? What does Rebecca need?” — proof and numbers, not vendor theater.
  • Debate: delay Tom’s proposal (E) vs win on merit with cost review + customer voice (B, K).
  • Emphasize customer-presented proof (Aisha to David) over battle cards (F) or proactive TCO decks (C).
Correct top 3: B · A · K

B — Cost optimization review: credible spend story for Rebecca.
A — Model serving demo with ML SSA, tailored to Meridian use cases.
K — Aisha presents ML wins to David: internal champion beats vendor narrative.

Not this round: Block Tom’s process (E), vendor TCO deck first (C), premature EBR (D), SQL Serverless POC before proof lands (H).

Quarter 4 — The Close

Read aloud after unlock The cost optimization review was a hit. You identified three hundred forty thousand dollars in annual savings through right-sizing and serverless SQL warehouses. Rebecca called it “the most useful vendor meeting I've had this year.” David shared the findings with his leadership team. Aisha presented ML results at the monthly data org all-hands: three times model training speedup, sixty percent cost reduction versus SageMaker, and a roadmap for model serving. After the meeting, two analytics engineers asked Marcus: “Can we use Databricks for our dashboards too?” Tom has withdrawn his Snowflake migration proposal. He still thinks Snowflake is better for pure analytics, but he can’t argue with the ML results or the cost savings. David is now asking about Databricks SQL Serverless and BI capabilities. Your AE shares that Rebecca wants to discuss right-sizing the commitment. She’s open to a two-year deal if the team can show a credible consumption growth plan tied to ML expansion and analytics modernization. The account has turned. The question is no longer “save the platform” — it’s grow trust, consumption, and commitment. What do you do now?
Shape the discussion
  • Contrast “celebrate / account saved” (L) with executive commercial moment (EBR + success plan).
  • David already signaled SQL Serverless curiosity — POC (A) beats full TS migration (D) this quarter.
  • SA role on EBR (C): frame value story and agenda; AE owns commercial.
Correct top 3: C · E · A

C — EBR with David, Rebecca, and AE: the CFO-facing commitment conversation.
E — Joint success plan mapping outcomes to Databricks capabilities.
A — Databricks SQL Serverless POC for analytics: next proof David asked for.

Not this round: Celebrate only (L), ignore Tom (I), forecast without exec forum (F), jump to full dashboard migration (D).

Discussion prompts (any round)

  • Diagnosis: “Who connected champion loss to the consumption story?”
  • Partial information: “Three separate problems or one arc?”
  • Escalation: “Was the best move not to escalate this quarter?”
  • Risk: “What could go wrong with your highest-leverage pick?”
  • Monday test: “What signal here would you watch on your own accounts?”
