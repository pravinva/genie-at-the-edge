Overview

SE pairs work through Meridian Health Systems across four rounds (Q1–Q4). Each round: read Events, discuss diagnosis offline, then each pair chooses their top three actions from that quarter’s lettered menu. After teams debrief aloud, you reveal GM ground truth from the source scenario bank — not from the app.

Learning objectives (from scenario bank)

  • Diagnose account-health signals and likely causes behind consumption changes
  • Prioritize actions under partial information and limited time
  • Know when to engage cross-functional partners (SSA, Technical Services, Support) vs when not to escalate
  • Shift mindset from “save the account” to “grow the account” as the story evolves
Scoring in the app: 3 points for each selected action that matches the optimal set for that round (max 9 per round, 36 total). After pairs report their three letters, enter them under each team in the sidebar and click Apply points for this team. The app shows how many points were recorded — not which letters were “supposed” to be correct.

Setup

  1. Open l3_sa_bootcamp.html?gm=1 on the facilitator / projector machine (Apps Script or GitHub Pages — add ?gm=1).
  2. Click Begin session → add a row per competing pair (only named rows count; use + Add team for extras) → Launch simulator.
  3. Explain: diagnosis is offline; the app only shows events + action menu; you will reveal ground truth verbally after each round.
Screen share: Everyone can watch the main board. Scoring: Only the facilitator (or co-facilitator) should click letter chips and Apply points so scores stay accurate. Participants can keep notes on paper — they do not need their own copy of the app unless you choose that format.
Two links (important):
• Facilitator / projector: open the same HTML file with ?gm=1 on the URL (example: …/l3_sa_bootcamp.html?gm=1). That unlocks Unlock round, Advance round, the scoreboard, and Apply points.
• Participants (optional): share the URL without ?gm=1. The intro lists read-only quarter links (?q=1 … ?q=4) so pairs can read events + action menu + account profile on their own devices — still no scores and no facilitator controls. Pairs change ?q= when you advance the room (or follow the projector only).
Re-scoring a team: If you change letter picks for the same round and click Apply points again, the total adjusts (it replaces this round’s points for that team — it does not stack duplicate +3s).

Controls & flow

  • Unlock this round — Reveals events, diagnosis instructions, and the action table.
  • Sidebar — For each team, tap exactly three action codes they committed to, then Apply points for this team. Points = 3 × (how many of their picks match the optimal set for that round). Wrong picks are ignored (no negative points).
  • Lock round → next quarter — After debrief and scoring, click Show scores → next quarter to put this-round points and running totals full-screen for the room, then continue. On Q4, the button reads Show scores → final standings.

Why these are correct — talking points

Q1 — A, K, E

Thesis: Three connected signals (champion exit, analytics SQL drop, Tom benchmarking) — fix coverage and learn the “why” before escalating or pitching.

  • A — 1:1 with Priya before she leaves (with AE): Champion departure is the highest-leverage, time-boxed risk. Capture who else owns the platform, who to land next, and transition intel before she walks. Partnering with AE keeps commercial + technical aligned.
  • K — Discovery with the analytics org on the usage drop: The 12% DBU drop is concentrated in SQL — you need firsthand diagnosis (workload moved? stalled? frustrated?) rather than guessing from Marcus alone or spinning up specialists too early.
  • E — Intro to Tom via Marcus: Competitive noise is real but still early. Hear Tom’s concerns directly (and through a trusted intro) instead of jumping to battle cards, UCOs, or Snowflake CI.
Common misses / anti-patterns: Manager escalate (C), EBR (F), SSA deep-dive (D), TS health check (I), CI/UCO (G/H) — premature; you lack a crisp ask. “Do nothing” (J) burns the Priya window. Urgent Marcus-only (B) or successor gossip (L) are useful later but don’t cover champion transfer + analytics root cause + Tom face-to-face.
Q2 — B, C, D

Thesis: New Snowflake-biased VP + ML growth + UC ticket storm. Discover before you pitch; nurture the bright spot; fix the structural platform pain with the right specialist.

  • B — Discovery questions for David (not a Snowflake-vs-Databricks pitch): He already framed spend as a problem. First meeting = listen (priorities, pain, vision). Pitch decks / “what Snowflake can’t do” (A/I) confirm the vendor stereotype he brought in.
  • C — Working session with Aisha / ML: Consumption growth is all ML — that’s your expansion story and proof point for David later. Learn what’s driving it, what’s next, and risk before dropping an ML SSA (L) or filing a UCO (F).
  • D — Technical Services for UC health / architecture: 14 tickets, 20% of Marcus’s team on UC permissions — pattern screams config/architecture, not “open more Support tickets” (K) or manager panic (G). TS is the right escalation once the pattern is clear (story validates this in Q3).
Common misses / anti-patterns: Competitive deck / value narrative for intro (A/I), early EBR with CFO (H), SQL workshop without knowing why analytics left (E), ML SSA before discovery (L). Triage with Marcus alone (J) is partial — the scale of UC pain warrants TS, not just ticket sorting.
Q3 — B, A, K

Thesis: David gave you two explicit asks (cost review + serving demo). Answer those with proof; amplify customer-owned ML wins. Don’t block Tom’s process or lead with vendor TCO theater.

  • B — Cost optimization review (current vs optimized): David needs numbers for Rebecca. Credible spend truth beats a proactive Databricks-vs-Snowflake TCO deck (C) or battle card (F). This is what later unlocks CFO trust.
  • A — Model serving demo with ML SSA, tailored to Meridian: He asked for it; discovery already showed depth beyond solo SA support. Bring the specialist for a use-case demo, not a generic product tour.
  • K — Aisha presents ML wins to David: Customer voice > vendor narrative. Internal champion proof (training speedup, cost vs SageMaker, roadmap) reframes the platform as growth, not a Snowflake migration debate.
Common misses / anti-patterns: Asking David to delay Tom’s proposal (E) — looks political and blocks their process. Vendor TCO / battle card first (C/F). Premature EBR (D) before the proof David requested. SQL Serverless POC / Tom therapy / more TS (H/G/L) can wait until cost + ML proof land. AE align / UCO (J/I) are hygiene, not the top-3 lever this quarter.
Q4 — C, E, A

Thesis: Account turned — shift from defend to grow. Convert momentum into an executive commitment story: EBR + joint success plan + a concrete analytics POC David already asked about.

  • C — EBR with David, Rebecca, and AE (SA frames value + agenda): Rebecca is open to a 2-year deal if growth is credible. This is the commercial moment; SA’s job is the value narrative and agenda, AE owns commercial.
  • E — Joint success plan (outcomes ↔ Databricks capabilities): Gives the EBR a durable artifact — ties ML expansion + analytics modernization to business outcomes the CFO will fund, not a one-off win lap.
  • A — Databricks SQL Serverless POC for analytics: David explicitly asked; analytics engineers are pulling toward Databricks after Aisha’s talk; Tom stood down but still prefers Snowflake for “pure analytics.” A POC is the right next proof, not a full TS migration yet (D).
Common misses / anti-patterns: Celebrate / account saved (L) or ignore Tom (I) — premature. Forecast-only for AE (F) or UCOs (K) without the exec forum and success plan. Jumping straight to TS dashboard migration / architecture review (D/J) before a POC proves appetite. Champions program / PM intro (H/G) are nice-to-haves after the commitment path is set. ML roadmap alone (B) without the CFO-facing package under-weights the commercial close.

Round-by-round facilitation

Q1 — The First Cracks

Focus discussion on connecting signals (champion exit, SQL drop, Tom’s benchmarks). After teams share their three actions, reveal ground truth from the PDF, then score in the app.

⏱️ Suggested: 12–18 min
Q2 — The Plot Thickens

New VP from a Snowflake-heavy background, ML up / SQL down, UC ticket storm. Good tension: discovery vs pitch, when TS is warranted, and when ML SSA is premature.

⏱️ Suggested: 12–18 min · Optional: 2-min brainstorm of discovery questions for David
Q3 — Turning Point

TS fixed UC; David wants serving demo + cost review; Tom’s proposal lands. Debrief influence without blocking customer process and credible numbers vs vendor TCO decks.

⏱️ Suggested: 12–18 min
Q4 — The Close

Account has turned; choices shift to EBR + joint success plan + SQL Serverless POC. Contrast with “celebrate only” or over-rotating to TS migration too early.

⏱️ Suggested: 12–18 min

Scoring reference

  • Per pick: +3 if the letter is one of the three optimal actions for that round.
  • Cap: 9 points per round per team; 36 overall.
  • Ties: Allowed — standings screen orders by total.

Discussion prompts (from scenario bank)

Use these after teams share their three actions and you reveal ground truth:

  • Diagnosis: “Who connected champion loss to the consumption story? Walk us through that reasoning.”
  • Partial information: “Did anyone treat these as three separate problems vs one arc?”
  • Actions: “Pair A pulled in an SSA early; Pair B did not — both can be argued. Make your case.”
  • Risk: “What could go wrong with the highest-leverage move you chose?”
  • Escalation judgment: “Was this a round where the best move was not to escalate? Who got that?”
  • Discovery (Q2): “You have 30 minutes with David — top three questions, no product names.”
  • Reflection: “What signal from this quarter would you watch on your own accounts Monday?”

Time management

Full run (4 rounds): about 55–80 minutes (12–18 min per round + transitions).
Tight agenda: shorten pair discussion to ~5 min and debrief to ~5 min per round (~45 min total).
  • Read events aloud (optional) while the slide is unlocked — 1–2 min.
  • Pair work — diagnosis + agree on three letters — 5–8 min.
  • Share-outs — a few teams report — 4–6 min.
  • GM reveal + debate — use PDF narrative — 5–8 min.
  • Enter scores in sidebar — 1–2 min.

Checklist

Before: Simulator opens · teams decided · PDF / answer key ready · timer optional
During: Unlock → discuss → share → verbal reveal → enter three letters per team → Apply points → next round
After: Show standings screen · recap themes · collect feedback

Deploying (Apps Script)

  2. Redeploy the web app so the new HTML is live at your script.google.com/.../exec URL.
  3. Keep l3_sa_bootcamp_facilitator_guide.html alongside it if you link facilitators to the guide from the same folder/host.
